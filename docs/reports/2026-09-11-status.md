## 3. KT1, where it stands

Preconditions all met (contract, checks, database, Reclaim credentials verified against Reclaim's
backend, preflight). Legs not yet run. The funder is sending about 1,500 MON from their own wallet to
the funder account; then: swap to AUSD (Kuru), gift of $20.00 over 7 days at 10 XP per day, claim by a
second passkey account, baseline check-in through Reclaim, daily check-ins, withdraw, one skipped day,
keeper drain, refund, send-back. The record is `docs/spikes/KT1.md`, legs 1b to 9, 10b, 11, 12.

Estimated fees if the consumer rail were used for a 20 EUR gift: 0.76 EUR in, 3 EUR out, 19 %. Domestic
web2 transfers cost 0 EUR; PayPal by card 2.9 % + 0.30 EUR; World Bank average for cross-border 6.49 %.
Consequence already in the rules: never claim "cheaper than a transfer". The value is the conditional
release, the automatic refund and the third-party funder; cost is a secondary benefit only on expensive
corridors and only if measured.

## 4. Findings that change the picture

1. **Fiat rails.** The two rails that remove the "buy MON, paste an address" screen both depend on a
   third party and neither has been requested: Calm (bank transfer paying AUSD, tenant by call) and a
   Mercuryo partner widget (application, KYB, sales manager; URL parameters are ignored without a
   `widget_id`, checked 11 Sep). The EUR leg of Mercuryo's widget is operated by CRIPTAN TRADE S.L.
   Payout on Mercuryo Sell goes to a card (Visa Direct, "minutes"); the IBAN route needs a Mercuryo
   Wallet whose EEA registrations are paused.
2. **Agora's stack (D23).** The bounty page says "build against Agora's public API documentation and
   staging environment", judges "business viability of the payments flow", and wants "a completed
   send/receive transaction settled instantly". Agora documents a Public API (`api.agora.finance`,
   organisation keys, USD-only bank accounts with US routing numbers, no staging documented) and an
   **Instant Settlement** fixed-price AUSD/USDC pair, live on Monad mainnet
   (`0xf33286E3…2470`, 5.35 M AUSD and 2.55 M USDC in reserve, 10 AUSD quoted 10 USDC, purchase fees
   0, swaps restricted to whitelisted addresses per the docs). Neither was in the spec or the plan.
   "Instant settlement" plausibly means their product, not only Monad finality.
3. **Mera UX bounty criteria** (read 11 Sep): time-to-first-transaction in taps and seconds; session
   design (prompt-free scope, clean expiry); the **stateless test** (judges clear local storage or use
   a fresh device mid-demo; identity must reconstruct from the passkey); prompt-free signing sessions;
   bonus for gas sponsorship, intents, recovery. The code passes the stateless test by design (sign-in
   falls back to the platform picker; gifts are found by account server-side); a "my gifts" screen and
   a visible session scope are missing.
4. **Community Team bounty** ($5,000, no code): eligibility is a campus or community group selected in
   the profile's community field on the portal. To check in the funder's profile.
5. **Envio bounty** deliverables: a deployed indexer with public `config.yaml`, `schema.graphql` and
   handlers, a front that consumes it, a demo; judged on depth (derived entities, aggregates).
6. **Track wording differs** between pages: "users who don't identify as crypto users" (tracks) versus
   "crypto-native users or anyone else" (prizes). $30,000 per track, three winners, split not
   published; Grand Champion $25,000.
7. **Consumer-track resources**: eight ideas, identical to the 8 Sep reading; nothing added.
8. **Support page** renders empty; KT2 (does a PWA count as a "mobile app") is still unanswered.
